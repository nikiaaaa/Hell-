# "The Clocks Came Down": original script v1 (2026-11-24)

Format: 9:16 vertical short, about 60 s, about 3.6 words/s. Flat 2D cartoon characters (round white heads, thin dark
stick limbs) in a 3D world, deadpan narrator, word-by-word captions. Story, characters and voice are our own.

## Facts and sources
- Popular theory: casinos remove clocks and daylight so players lose track of time. The Horseshoe Casino GM calls this the
  "conspiracy theory". (Mental Floss, "Why casinos don't have clocks")
- Nevada legalized gambling in 1931, and the casinos then did have clocks. The biggest gamblers complained and asked for
  them to be removed, threatening not to come back. Casino expert Bill Friedman calls the time-loss idea a "myth".
  (Mental Floss; casino.org "Vegas Myths Busted")
- Psychologist Mark Griffiths reviewed more than 15 studies on casino design and found no conclusive research on the
  effect of clocks or windows on gamblers. (Mental Floss)
- Today it hardly matters: everyone carries a clock in their pocket. (Mental Floss)
- The "lost his ranch at three in the morning" gambler is an illustrative example, not a documented person.

## Voice-over
Casinos don't have clocks. And everybody knows why.
They want you to lose track of time.
No clocks. No windows. Just you, a slot machine, and a carpet that looks like a headache.
But it didn't start that way.
In nineteen thirty-one, Nevada makes gambling legal, and the first casinos hang big clocks right on the wall.
Then the biggest gamblers start to complain.
A man who just lost his ranch at three in the morning does not want a clock telling him it's three in the morning.
So the high rollers make a deal. Take the clocks down, or we stop coming.
And down they came.
Luckily, the casinos listened.
Unluckily, they listened to the people with the most money.
Think of the clock in a hospital waiting room. Nobody has ever looked at it and felt better.
Years later, a psychologist went through more than fifteen studies on casino design, and found no real proof that missing clocks keep anyone playing.
So the casinos never hid the time from you.
The gamblers hid it from themselves.
And today, it doesn't matter. The clock is in your pocket. Right next to the money you're about to lose.
